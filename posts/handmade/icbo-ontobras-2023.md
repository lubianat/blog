<p>Last week I had the great opportunity to join two conferences at the same time: <strong>ICBO</strong> and
<strong>Ontobras</strong>!
</p>

<p>I worked on two different sessions:
<ul>
<li>The <a href="https://oboacademy.github.io/obook/courses/icbo2023/" target="_blank">OBO Academy
tutorial</a>, alongside Nicolas Matentzoglu, Nicole Vasilevsky, and others.</li>
<li>The <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/cells2023" target="_blank">CELLS workshop on cells in the
experimental life sciences</a>, with Alexander Diehl, David Osumi-Sutherland, and others.</li>
</ul>
Both sessions brought up fruitful discussions and lots of learning.</p>

<p>I also had the opportunity to present a poster on <a href="https://zenodo.org/records/8284498"
target="_blank">connecting Wikidata and the Cell Ontology</a> and spread the word about Wikidata
alongside Erika Guetti (pictured below with the Wiki Movimento Brasil flag).</p>

<p>It was outstanding to connect with the Brazilian ontology community, including César Bernabé, Claudenir
Fonseca, Prof. Giancarlo Guizzardi, and others. It made me proud of my origins in the state of Espírito
Santo! Amazing what the <a href="https://nemo.inf.ufes.br/" target="_blank">NEMO research group</a> has
constructed over the years.</p>

<p>Finally, I had inspiring conversations with researchers whose work I've read so much, especially Barry
Smith (virtually) and Stefan Schulz. I’m sure that from now on, I’ll read their articles with a new
perspective in mind.</p>

<p>A big thanks to Fernanda Farinelli, Amanda Damasceno de Souza, and all others involved in the
organization!</p>
